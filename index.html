About ARIES

An independent society, built around a method.

ARIES was formed to give serious research a home outside university funding cycles and startup timelines — where a question can take as long as it needs.

Most independent researchers face the same obstacle: institutions willing to fund a two-year study rarely tolerate a finding that takes three. ARIES was founded in 2023 as the research division of AMAZE Consortium to remove that constraint — providing structure, peer review, and modest funding to researchers working outside traditional academic or corporate settings, while asking nothing of them but rigor.

In practice: every study accepted into ARIES is assigned a review pair — one member inside the researcher's field, one deliberately outside it — before work begins, not after.

An external advisory board of eleven researchers, unaffiliated with ARIES day-to-day operations, reviews contested findings on request. Their dissent is published alongside the work, not filed separately.

ARIES operates as the research division of AMAZE Consortium, which provides administrative and funding infrastructure. Editorial and research decisions are made independently by ARIES leadership and its review board. For matters unrelated to research — consortium-wide partnerships, corporate inquiries — visit amazeconsortium.org.

Method

Four stages, no exceptions.

01

Inquiry

Proposals must state a falsifiable question. We reject roughly 40% of submissions at this stage — most commonly because the question already assumes its answer.

02

Evidence

Data is logged in a shared, timestamped record as it's collected — not reconstructed afterward from notes.

03

Review

A specialist reviewer checks the method; a generalist reviewer checks whether the conclusion actually follows from the data.

04

Publication

Papers are released with raw data, review notes, and any unresolved disagreement between reviewers.

Featured — March 2026

Mapping tidal erosion along the Sundarbans coastline

R. Chowdhury, Lead Field Researcher, ARIES Coastal Systems Group

Over twenty-two months, four monitoring stations tracked sediment displacement across a twelve-kilometer stretch of mangrove coastline. Erosion accelerated non-linearly in zones adjacent to root systems damaged by upstream dredging, suggesting root density functions as a threshold variable rather than a linear buffer.

Additional Authors: M. Islam · A. Osei · S. Rahman · T. Banerjee · K. Huda

Field access supported by three coastal fishing cooperatives; equipment funded by AMAZE Consortium's Field Research Grant.

Every published study is accompanied by its underlying dataset, released under an open license unless a participant's privacy requires otherwise. We would rather a finding be wrong in public than unverifiable in private.

Case Study

Failure modes in municipal water systems

Eleven months into a field survey across three municipal water networks, the team has documented forty-two distinct failure events. The pattern emerging so far: failures cluster around administrative handoffs between maintenance shifts, not around equipment age, which was the initial hypothesis.

Current phase: cross-referencing failure logs against staffing schedules.

Field Notes

What eleven months in a mangrove forest teaches you about patience

Field researcher Rana Chowdhury on the difference between a dataset that looks finished and one that actually is.

By Rana Chowdhury · July 2026 · 6 min read

The instinct, three months into any field study, is to see a pattern early and start writing toward it. The Sundarbans project taught us to distrust that instinct specifically around month four — which is exactly when the data starts to look convincing and is exactly when it's usually still wrong.

Continue reading →

By month eleven, two of the four monitoring stations had been rebuilt after storm damage, and the dataset that finally held up looked nothing like the one the team was ready to publish in month four. The lesson wasn't to work faster — it was to build review checkpoints early enough to catch a convincing story before it hardens into a conclusion.

Community

A network held together by standards, not proximity.

ARIES members are spread across nineteen countries. What connects them is a shared review process, not a shared building.

We don't organize social events. Every gathering — in person or virtual — exists to move a piece of research forward: a working session, a field briefing, an open review where a paper's conclusions are argued in front of the room before publication.
